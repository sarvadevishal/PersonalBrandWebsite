Genpact: 79% run AI agents in production, 15% tie AI spend to P&L

A Genpact survey of more than 2,000 executives found 79% have AI agents in production, but only 20% use them broadly and just 15% have fully tied AI investment to P&L outcomes.

ROIEnterprise adoption

On November 12, Genpact released Autonomy in Practice, a survey of more than 2,000 senior executives at companies with annual revenue from $1 billion to more than $50 billion, across 17 industries and 14 business functions1. The headline gap is between deployment and payoff. Genpact reports that 79% of enterprises have AI agents in production, but only 20% use them broadly and consistently across functions, and just 15% have fully tied AI investment to P&L outcomes1. On average, only 49% of AI initiatives funded in the past 24 months delivered the value expected1.

Cryptonomist's coverage of the study adds the decision picture. AI now leads or fully handles 40% of decisions, more than double the share a year earlier, yet people still make or audit decisions in at least 87% of cases at each stage2. Only 9% of respondents have completed substantial changes to their operating models, and 92% of those with agents in production kept human headcount the same or grew it2. Genpact sells agentic operations services, and these are executive self-assessments, so treat the exact figures with care.

The hard part is no longer deployment. It is proving what an agent earned. A company can count agents, but if fewer than one in six can trace AI spend to a P&L line, most value claims rest on estimates, and half of funded projects are falling short of their own targets1.

That tracing work lands on data teams. To tie an agent to a P&L outcome, we need a baseline for the process before the agent arrived, an event record of what the agent did, and a join between those runs and the business metric they affect. That means agent actions logged as data with lineage back to source tables, cost tagged per agent and use case, and quality checks on the outcome metric itself. Without that, a finance team cannot audit the claim.

One step this week: pick one agent already in production and write down the single business metric it is supposed to move. Then check whether you can query the metric before and after launch and join it to the agent's run logs and compute cost. If you cannot, that missing join is the first thing to build.
