Agent governance: confidence is high, controls are not

A new survey of 858 executives finds 82% believe they can govern AI agents. Only 5% have automated controls, audit trails and human review points in place.

GovernanceEnterprise adoption

A survey published this week by OutSystems, with CIO Dive and KPMG, asked 858 executives how ready they are to run AI agents. 82% said their organizations can keep pace with governing, integrating and monitoring agents. Only 5% have automated controls, audit trails and formal human review points in place1.

Other research points the same way. Deloitte's 2026 State of AI in the Enterprise survey of 3,235 leaders found that 21% have a mature governance model for agentic AI2. IBM's Institute for Business Value, as reported by Analytics India Magazine, found 77% of respondents say AI adoption is outpacing their governance capabilities3.

Most teams are stretching what they already have. 83% of the OutSystems respondents oversee agents through existing IT, security or compliance processes1. That holds up while an agent drafts text for a person to approve. It breaks once the agent writes to a system of record, hands work to another agent, or runs on a schedule nobody is watching.

Data teams have solved a version of this before. We already run unattended jobs we can trust: each one has an owner, a record of what it read and wrote, a way to rerun it, and an alert when it misbehaves. Agents need the same four things before they get write access. The survey also shows where the pain lands first: 35% named data consistency and context sharing as a major orchestration hurdle1.

The first step is small. Pick one agent that is already in production and list every system it can touch. Then check whether you can answer three questions about yesterday's run: what did it do, who approved it, and how would you undo it. Any question you can't answer is your governance backlog.
