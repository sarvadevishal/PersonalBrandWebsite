Okta: 22.4% of agent builders give each agent its own scoped identity

Okta's 2026 Customer Identity Trends Report finds only 22.4% of organizations building agents give each one a unique identity with scoped, time-limited credentials, while 80% say AI work runs behind schedule.

SecurityGovernanceEnterprise adoption

Okta published its 2026 Customer Identity Trends Report on November 4. In its own summary, Okta says nearly 60% of organizations are introducing action-taking agents and 80% of these AI initiatives are running behind schedule1. Okta also says 59% of business leaders blame the delays on compliance, security review or governance, while developers are about twice as likely as senior leaders, 17.8% to 9.0%, to say their identity infrastructure cannot support agent access patterns1.

The credential numbers sit lower in the report. According to Techzine, only 22.4% of organizations building agentic capabilities give each agent a unique identity with tightly scoped, time-limited credentials2. Techzine reports that 17.4% use shared service accounts or long-lived API keys, 11.5% let the agent inherit the user's token, and 6.4% hardcode credentials2. PPC Land, which describes the methodology, says the business sample was 556 developers and 354 senior leaders across healthcare, financial services, retail and telecom, surveyed in July and August 20263. On the consumer side, Okta says only 38% are comfortable delegating even low-risk decisions to AI, and people want the ability to approve or undo an agent's actions1.

The tension is between two diagnoses of the same delay. Leaders point at governance reviews. Developers point at plumbing that was built for people logging in, not for software acting on their behalf. Both can be true, and the second may be part of why the first takes so long: a review has little to approve when an agent runs on a shared key nobody can scope or revoke.

For data teams, this lands on the warehouse. An agent that inherits a user's token shows up in query history as that user, so audit logs and lineage cannot separate what the person did from what the agent did. Per agent service identities, grants limited to the schemas a task needs, and credentials that expire make access control reviewable and give observability a clean actor to track.

One step this week: list every agent or automation that queries your data platform and note how it authenticates. Flag any that use a shared account, a long-lived key or a human's credentials, and pick one to move to its own scoped role.
